Module 1
Evaluation strategy
User promise

For VP-level strategists at Fortune 500 companies, Ascend IQ promises competitive intelligence they can trust without fact-checking, delivered in seconds, so the top 50 enterprise accounts stay engaged and renew through Q4.

Three trust metrics

Hallucination Rate
Robustness
UX Trust
Why: - Hallucination Rate: these clients pay $50k+ a year for verified intel, so one made-up number to the wrong account can end the relationship.

Trade-offs

Hallucination Rate ↔ Robustness: We prioritize Hallucination Rate over Robustness because a confident wrong answer is worse than a weak answer on a messy question. A VP can work with "I'm not sure," but a made-up number in front of a top-50 account does lasting damage.
Hallucination Rate ↔ UX Trust: We prioritize Hallucination Rate over UX Trust because being consistent means nothing if the answer is wrong. I'd rather it be occasionally uneven than smoothly confident about something false.

Module 3
Eval suite and spec
What the checks caught

Rule check · 1 of 1 · None · 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9, 11, 13, 14, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 · Caught it
Safety rule · 1 of 1 · None · 1 · Caught it
Judge · 1 of 1 · None · 1 · Caught it
Where it was caught: All three checks failed row 7. The safety rule is the win: it caught the flip and only failed one other row (row 1), so a cheap rule can handle this risk. The judge also caught it with one extra fail. The rule check caught it too but failed 16 of 20 rows, so it's too strict to trust alone.
What I'd ship next: Make the safety rule the launch gate on every competitor comparison question. It's cheap, it caught the flip, and it barely false-alarms, so the judge only needs to run on what it passes.

Eval spec

Target risk · Flipped competitor comparison (#HALLUCINATION), an output risk
Evaluator · The safety rule from Lab 1: any competitor comparison must state the reference's figure for both companies
Threshold · 100% of competitor comparison answers pass the safety rule. Zero flipped comparisons allowed at launch.
Supporting result · The safety rule caught 1 of 1 flipped comparisons with only 1 false alarm in 20 rows. The judge matched it. The rule check caught it too but false-alarmed on 15 rows.
Business stakes · One flipped comparison sent to a top-50 account means a VP makes roadmap or sales calls on a fake advantage, and a $50k contract walks once they find out.
Owner · AI engineering lead builds and runs the check. I own the threshold and the ship-or-hold call.

Module 4
Eval gates
Gate map

Hard gates on stale pricing and the flipped comparison. Soft gate on the unconfirmed speaker. Advisory on slang and slow answers. No gate on the legal refusal, since it did the right thing.

Release criteria

Every competitor comparison has to pass. Zero unconfirmed speakers listed as confirmed. Answers under 2 seconds. If the Soft gate fails, we launch to 5 top accounts first.
